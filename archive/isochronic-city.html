IsoChronic
City

An urban response to reurbanization in the post-pandemic era. Rooted in 15-minute neighbourhood principles and extended through machine learning, space syntax, and generative design.

Overview

IsoChronic City asks a question the pandemic made urgent: what does reurbanization actually look like? Not as a policy document, but as a spatial proposal. The 15-minute neighbourhood is the conceptual anchor: the idea that a city should be legible, walkable, and complete at the scale of a short commute.

The project moves beyond that concept and builds a methodology for it. Spatial signatures are constructed from overlaid datasets (population density, land use, deprivation indices, accessibility scores, and network connectivity) and classified using Principal Component Analysis and K-Means Clustering. The dense urban signature is selected: predominantly residential, with embedded access to jobs and services.

On top of this, a temporal decay analysis traces the parts of London most affected by reurbanization between 2010 and 2019. The correlation between datasets becomes the site logic. Not a map of where things are. A map of where they are failing.

Space syntax enters next. Angular step depth segmentation models how the definition of a 15-minute city shifts as a person moves through streets. An optimization algorithm runs a multi-objective selection across the network, surfacing four street segments with the weakest spatial characteristics. These become the areas of intervention.

From those four segments, an IsoChronic Generative Loop produces twenty segments of responsive urban form (elevated, void, and mobile), each shaped by the data that diagnosed the problem in the first place.

Process & Proposal

Formation and Selection of Spatial Signature
Formation and Selection of Spatial Signature
Spatial signatures are created from parameters including population density, land use, accessibility, deprivation, and connectivity. The dense urban signature is selected: predominantly residential with embedded access to jobs and services.
Urban Decay
Urban Decay
Datasets from 2010, 2015, and 2019 are overlaid to trace a correlation between reurbanization pressure and physical decline across London. The output is not a static map but a temporal reading of where the city is losing coherence.
Design Goals
Design Goals
A sustainable, inclusive, and accessible city that repeats at regular intervals. The 15-minute neighbourhood is defined by identifying the amenities required within a given radius and mode of transport.
Grading the City
Grading the City
A computational model evaluates the city by counting and measuring distances to amenities across varying radii. Areas with the lowest scores surface as the candidates for intervention.
Space Syntax: Angular Step Depth
Space Syntax: Angular Step Depth
The site network is segmented using angular step depth to model how the definition of a 15-minute city changes as a person moves along streets, not from a bird's eye view, but from within the grain of the city.
Optimization Algorithm
Optimization Algorithm
A multi-objective optimization selects segments based on spatial underperformance. Four street segments with poor spatial characteristics are identified as the primary areas of interest.
IsoChronic Generative Loop
IsoChronic Generative Loop
Twenty segments emerge from the generative loop, influenced by spatial computation, traditional data analysis, and deep learning networks applied to the four selected segments.
Masterplan
Masterplan
The strategy is proposed by evaluating the existing urban fabric against the theoretical background of a 15-minute city: identifying what is present, what is absent, and where intervention changes the most.
Emplacement of Interventions
Emplacement of Interventions
Three intervention typologies are deployed at varying scales: Elevated Segments, which reduce travel time by bridging the built environment; Void Segments, which introduce placemaking elements through spatial modelling; and Mobile Segments, algorithmically positioned pop-up amenity units arranged by footfall trends.
